## Contract and reference lens

- The controlling outline behavior is the HTML source's `fill: none`: one to three closely displaced, continuous circular strokes with a clearly empty center, related gradient color, moderate transparency, small spacing, and small wobble.
- Outline must remain visibly different from counterform (a body with a cut center), halo, filled torus, ripple disc, target, or sticker.
- The material spec requires one-color ownership to stay coherent and two-/three-color ownership to arise from broad, overlapping radial influence with smooth transitions and no angular, conic, pyramidal, Voronoi-like, or hard sticker boundary.
- The reference board favors atmospheric transparency, differentiated focus/depth, cropped continuation beyond the frame, and saturated-but-soft color fields. Composition is frozen for this comparison and is not scored here.

## Locked per-condition observations

### c1-light

- **A full + tile:** Clear thin-outline identity. Centers remain empty at every visible scale, including the very large lower crop and the small left cluster. Strokes are continuous and softly transparent; the large strokes thicken modestly with scale but do not become bodies. Color stays on one pale lime/olive axis with gentle modulation. The tile preserves the same open geometry without a new inner ring or crop discontinuity. Some top/right contours are very faint against the warm light field, but their topology remains open.
- **B full + tile:** Multiple actors become broad glossy toruses; the upper-right actor is a filled concentric ripple/target disc. Small actors also read as layered doughnuts or dots. Hard green edge fringes, bright internal rims, and repeated sharp inner circles override the intended open-contour construction. The tile makes the target/ripple behavior more dominant.
- **Blind preference:** A.

### c1-dark

- **A full + tile:** Every discernible actor remains an open, continuous contour. The large lower crop feels translucent and atmospheric rather than sticker-like, and the tile retains edge continuity. However, contrast is fragile: the top, upper-right, and portions of the upper-left cluster approach disappearance on the dark ground, making several contours less than unmistakable at native size.
- **B full + tile:** Stronger raw visibility comes from thick shaded ring bodies rather than thin strokes. The left and lower actors are toruses with hard inner rims; the upper-right is a filled ripple/target with a sharp internal halo circle. The material reads embossed and opaque, not like the source outline family.
- **Blind preference:** A, despite its low-contrast weakness.

### c1-lowContrast

- **A full + tile:** Broad lime bodies, pink/green edge fringes, internal shading, and concentric rings turn the family into toruses and targets. The upper-right disc is especially explicit as a ripple target, while several small actors become thick counterform-like rings. The tile reinforces the filled/glazed impression.
- **B full + tile:** Thin open contours survive throughout. The olive/lime one-color identity is coherent, transitions are soft, and there is no sharp inner halo circle. The low-contrast presentation is deliberately restrained yet remains more readable than the darkest condition; the large bottom crop and central rings stay continuous in the tile.
- **Blind preference:** B.

### c2-light

Cyan/green/lime ownership appears as broad, smooth variation along the contours, with no angular seam or isolated sticker patch. Closely displaced strokes read as related multi-outline wobble rather than a thick torus. The tile keeps the overlaps transparent and the cropped arcs continuous. A few pale actors remain near the visibility floor.
- **B full + tile:** The two-color treatment is vivid but materially wrong: broad glazed ring bodies, bright inner rims, white/green highlights, and the upper-right ripple disc create torus/target identities. Fine concentric lines and hard inner-circle boundaries are especially conspicuous in the tile.
- **Blind preference:** A.

### c2-dark

Dark internal bands, sharp inner rims, and concentric ripples make the objects feel embossed and opaque. Open holes in some actors do not rescue outline identity because the ring body itself is broad and volumetric.
- **B full + tile:** The family returns to thin open contours with continuous edges and no sharp inner halo circle. Green-to-yellow variation is smooth and related. Transparency and depth softness feel appropriate, but several contours—especially top and upper-right—are so dim that native-size identity is close to a missing-contour failure. The tile preserves topology where the strokes remain visible.
- **Blind preference:** B.

### c2-lowContrast

- **A full + tile:** Thin, open, continuous contours are clear across the crop. Cyan/green and yellow contributions are visibly distinct without a hard ownership edge, and the large bottom arc retains translucent depth rather than becoming a banded body. The tile shows clean overlap and uninterrupted cropped arcs. Peripheral actors are subdued but still read as members of the same outline family.
- **B full + tile:** Thick multicolor toruses, internal glaze, hard chromatic fringes, and a filled upper-right ripple target dominate. Some small shapes resemble counterforms because their broad bodies surround small cut centers. The tile exposes the hard inner-circle treatment and sticker-like polish.
- **Blind preference:** A.

### c3-light

The chief weakness is color ownership: the nominal three-color condition reads overwhelmingly periwinkle/blue with only tonal variation, so distinct second and third color contributions are not convincingly visible in either full or tile view. ### c3-lowContrast

Centers stay empty, strokes remain continuous, overlaps are translucent, and cropped arcs continue cleanly through the tile. No torus, target, sticker field, sharp inner halo, or angular seam appears. A few peripheral actors are intentionally faint but the central and large actors retain clear identity.
- **B full + tile:** The same colors are more saturated but live in broad torus bodies, hard-edged internal bands, and the filled upper-right ripple target. Pink/purple edge fringes and glossy inner rims add sticker-like separation. The tile retains the material failure rather than resolving it.
- **Blind preference:** A.

## Overall blind preference

Prefer the **thin open-contour presentation in every condition**. Because the packet's labels vary by condition, that corresponds to **A in 6/9 conditions** (`c1-light`, `c1-dark`, `c2-light`, `c2-lowContrast`, `c3-dark`, `c3-lowContrast`) and **B in 3/9 conditions** (`c1-lowContrast`, `c2-dark`, `c3-light`).

Across all nine pairs, the thin presentation consistently removes the erroneous sharp inner halo circle and restores outline/counterform family distinction. It also avoids torus, ripple, target, sticker, and angular-seam readings, while preserving smooth transparent depth and clean full-to-tile edge continuity. The competing presentation is more saturated and sometimes more immediately visible, but its visibility is achieved by replacing a contour with a glossy ring body or concentric filled disc.

The locked residual concerns in the preferred thin presentation are native-size visibility on dark backgrounds (most notably `c1-dark`, `c2-dark`, and `c3-dark`), near-disappearance of some pale peripheral actors on light backgrounds, and weakly demonstrated three-color ownership in `c3-light` and `c3-dark`. No PASS/ITERATE judgment is recorded in this blind phase.
